# Analysis for Job 3033299 (pilot_cpu_phydll_py_packed)

## Investigation findings

The job did NOT fail due to a computation error. The workload completed 100% successfully, and the job was then killed during teardown, which is why Slurm recorded CANCELLED (exit 0:0) instead of COMPLETED.

### What succeeded
- All 22 simulation steps ran to completion (alternating Regular/ML solver steps, log lines ~375-1495). No errors, exceptions, or MPI aborts anywhere in the log.
- The final trajectory was fully written: `Finished. Saved snapshots: 2 to /hpcwork/thes2181/mini_app/pilot_cpu_phydll_py_packed/world_trajectory.h5` (line 1498). The file exists on disk, 4.76 MB, timestamp 03:39.
- Clean teardown on both sides: `[shutdown] entering MPI_Comm_free / MPI_Finalize` (1499), `(PhyDLL:PHY) ----> Finalize... ...done` (1506), `[DL] Exited MPI.Finalize()` (1573). Peak RSS ~274 MB/rank, healthy.

### Where/why it was killed
- Job `pilot_cpu_phydll_py_packed` (3033299, case 10 of run_pilot_benchmark_suite.sh, USE_PYTHON_DL_CLIENT=1) runs the solver (8 ranks, het-group 0) and the Python PhyDLL DL client (1 rank, het-group 1) in a single MPMD `srun` with colon syntax (proper_slurm_job.sh lines 1952-1983). In a hetjob, each component is a separate Slurm job: 3033299 = solver component, 3033300 = DL-client component.
- Final log lines (1574-1580): `srun: First task exited 5s ago`, `srun: StepId=3033299.1 tasks 0-7: exited` (solver step done), `srun: StepId=3033300.1 task 0: running` (DL task STILL RUNNING), `srun: Terminating StepId=3033299.1 / 3033300.1`, `srun: Job step aborted: Waiting up to 92 seconds...`, `error: *** JOB 3033299 ON n23m0003 CANCELLED AT 2026-08-18T03:39:28 DUE to SIGNAL Terminated ***`.
- Root cause: after the solver step exits, the Python DL client process hangs during Python interpreter shutdown, even though it printed `[DL] Exited MPI.Finalize()` (1573). A lingering non-daemon background thread (PyTorch runtime; log line 291 shows `torch threads: intra=8`) blocks interpreter exit, so the MPMD step never completes. The 5s gap matches the artificial `time.sleep(5)` in `phydll_dl_client.py` line 567 (after the `PHYDLL_MPMD_SHUTDOWN_BARRIER` barrier at line 565), which kept the DL rank alive while the solver tore down.
- The canceller UID 35572 is the job owner `ro092286` (verified via `getent passwd 35572`). There is no `scancel`/`kill`/timeout logic in proper_slurm_job.sh or run_pilot_benchmark_suite.sh, and the 20-min limit was not reached (job ran ~7 min). So this is a self-inflicted termination during teardown attributed to the owner, not a manual external cancel.

### Reproducible, code-path-specific
An identical analysis exists for the sibling case: `output_3033302.txt.analysis.md` documents the exact same signature for `pilot_cpu_phydll_py_uniform` (job 3033302, cancelled 03:39:55). The C++-DL-client PhyDLL cases (3033293, 3033296) and all other CPU cases in the suite completed cleanly with a `Total time` summary — only the two Python-DL-client cases exhibit this hang.

### Remedies / next steps
1. In `phydll_dl_client.py`, after `MPI.Finalize()` (line 585), call `os._exit(0)` to bypass the interpreter-shutdown deadlock on lingering non-daemon threads (standard MPI+Python workaround).
2. Reduce the hang surface: set `torch.set_num_threads(1)` / `torch.set_num_interop_threads(1)` before model load; diagnose the stuck thread with `PYTHONFAULTHANDLER=1` or `py-spy dump` on the DL process right after `Exited MPI.Finalize()`.
3. Optionally remove the now-redundant `time.sleep(5)` at `phydll_dl_client.py:567` (the C++ client completes cleanly without it).
4. As a batch-level safety net, wrap the srun eval in a watchdog that SIGKILLs the hetjob a few seconds after the physical step exits so accounting records COMPLETED.

Impact: any automation keying on COMPLETED vs CANCELLED flags these two Python-DL runs as failed even though the scientific output is valid; the 22-step workload itself needs no re-run.
